# Peer-review report (ACM TOSEM polish, Devil's Advocate + ARS)

Reviewer mode: pre-submission strict (Devil's Advocate + Reviewer-2 ARS).
Target paper: `NOETHER_paper.tex` (2697 lines, 71 pp).
Scan basis: §1 Introduction through §C.7 Appendix; tables (1-8), Hypothesis H1-H4, Propositions on PWR.

## A. Devil's Advocate (论证一致性)

### 致命论证问题 (publication blocker)

- **A-PB1. D2-stratum denominator inconsistency (0/5 vs 0/7)**, internal numerical contradiction. Lines 1203–1209, 1690–1694, and Table footnote at line 1763 fix the post-equivalent-mutant D2 stratum at `n = 5` with Set N = 0/5 (Wilson 95% CI [0.000, 0.434]). Yet the same prediction-pass narrative is restated in lines 1815 and 2078 as **"Set~N = 0/7"** within the `kill rate ≤ 10%` ceiling. The "0/7" in 1815/2078 reuses the *G-block on `gcdSig`+`lcmSig`* denominator (Table 7 line 1597, $n_G = 11$ with 7 Set-G-only kills on the Euclidean SUTs) — a D1 substrate readout — and conflates it with the D2 framework-prediction stratum. The paper's headline falsifiability test for Hypothesis H4 / Theorem 1's empirical-mechanism claim is therefore reported with two incompatible denominators in the same manuscript. Either (i) one of "0/5" and "0/7" is wrong, or (ii) the D2 stratum size shifted between revisions and §6.6.4 / §6 Summary were not updated. As-is, the central empirical commitment of §6 fails self-consistency.

- **A-PB2. The D2 ≤10% prediction "PASS" is not statistically supported at n=5.** Lines 1207-1209 declare "the prediction $\mathrm{kill\;rate}_{\mathrm{D2}} \le 10\%$ holds (PASS)" with Set N = 0/5 and Wilson 95% CI = [0.000, 0.434]. The CI upper bound at **0.434** is more than 4× the 10% prediction ceiling: the data are consistent with a true D2 kill rate as high as ~43%, so the test cannot reject the alternative hypothesis "framework prediction false (kill rate > 10%)" at n=5. Calling this a falsifiability-criterion PASS overclaims the inferential weight of an underpowered observation. The honest verdict is "0/5 observed, consistent with the prediction; n=5 insufficient to confirm at α=0.05 because CI[0,0.434] contains values above the 10% threshold." This is the exact escape-hatch C4 warns against: a small-n descriptive pattern dressed as a passed falsification test. A direct phrasing fix is required in lines 1207-1216, 1689-1693, 1813-1816, 2076-2080, and the abstract's claim that the framework's D2 prediction holds (line 78, "Structural-coverage predictions are consistent with the data within each case-study scope" is fine; if a stronger reading is asserted elsewhere it must be retracted).

- **A-PB3. Abstract under-claims relative to §6's confirmed `L*`-blindness and over-claims relative to §6's `n=5` pilot.** Abstract (line 78) says "Structural-coverage predictions are consistent with the data within each case-study scope; comparative evaluation against three SOTA representatives ... is reported alongside an algebra-aligned metric framework that distinguishes algebra-disrupting from algebra-preserving mutants" — but conceals (a) the §6.5 `L*`-blindness result, which is the paper's only confirmed quantitative falsifiable prediction, and (b) the fact that on the head-to-head **D1 stratum (n=52)** Set G **wins** (M1 = 0.712 vs 0.500, McNemar exact two-sided p = 0.019 / pooled p = 0.0043). Reading the abstract alone, a reviewer cannot tell that on the framework's primary fault-detection metric (D1 kill rate), the GP-evolved baseline beats Set N significantly. §6.10 line 1809-1817 correctly acknowledges this internally ("We do *not* claim Set~N $>$ Set~G on the scope-matched D1 head-to-head ... Set~G is the stronger fault-detector within the D1 scope-matched subset on this substrate"), but the abstract's "head-to-head superiority claim is not asserted" elides the directional finding *against* Set N on D1. A TOSEM reviewer will read this as concealment. Required fix: abstract must say either "Set G outperforms Set N on the algebra-disrupting D1 stratum (p = 0.019); the framework's contribution is therefore re-framed as per-block precision plus the D2 scope prediction" *or* "Set N matches Set G per-block on $\mathcal{T}^*$ and underperforms on $G$ on Euclidean-style SUTs."

### 严重但非致命

- **A-S1. C4 (cross-domain external validity) is mixed: it bundles (i) §5 equivariant ML (case study n=20 hand-constructed), (ii) §5.6.1 DeepCrime-style pilot (n=5), and (iii) Apache Commons Math 3.6.1 pilot ("b.cm", 3 SUTs, 5 MRs, 77 mutants in Table 9 / line 2024 / line 2102) into a single "transferability" claim**. The n=5 DeepCrime pilot p-value is reported as p=1.00 (line 755), and the commons-math pilot at lines 2102 is correctly tagged "underpowered for α=0.05 hypothesis testing", but C4 in line 136 reads as "the framework's structural-coverage prediction is consistent with the data within case-study scope" — without flagging that of three nominally independent external-validity instantiations, two are underpowered (n=5 each) and one is construct-validity-controlled (the 20-mutation case study). The construct-validity caveat is now stated four times across §5.6 (lines 714, 717, 760, 2100) — that part is honest. But C4's stand-alone reading should be tightened to: "case-study transferability (single architecture, n=20 mutations constructed for per-block coverage) + two n=5 pilots (DeepCrime EGNN, commons-math) consistent with framework predictions but underpowered for inferential conclusions."

- **A-S2. Hypothesis H1's downgrade from "falsifiable" to "structural sanity check" is *internally* consistent but tonally inconsistent with the pre-registration framing.** Lines 681-685 pre-register H1 ($\mathrm{coverage}_{\mathrm{NOETHER}}(N) = 1.0$ by construction) as a "pre-registered hypothesis". Lines 711-713 then announce: "H1 is **retained as a structural sanity check rather than as a falsifiable hypothesis test**. By the framework's construction, $\mathrm{coverage}_{\mathrm{NOETHER}}(N) = 1.00$ holds *before any experiment is run*". This is the correct technical reading, but pre-registering a non-falsifiable structural identity *as* a hypothesis weakens the credibility of the pre-registration protocol itself. The honest narrative is: "H1 was incorrectly stated as a hypothesis in our pre-registration; it is now restated as a structural-prior diagnostic." A short paragraph in §6.7 or §6.12 acknowledging the mis-classification would strengthen the integrity story.

- **A-S3. The `L*`-blindness result is genuinely operative but the abstract does not surface it.** §6.5 is the paper's most defensible empirical claim (5/6 SUTs confirmed at zero kill; 1/6 SUT outlier `hypotSig` with mechanistic explanation; prediction derivable ex-ante from public info). This is the strongest substrate-level evidence for the eight-block decomposition's "operative mechanism" reading. Yet the abstract makes no reference to it; the closest is the phrase "an algebra-aligned metric framework that distinguishes algebra-disrupting from algebra-preserving mutants" — which is the D1/D2 stratification, not `L*`-blindness. A single sentence in the abstract reporting the §6.5 result (e.g., "An ex-ante derivable prediction—$L_{\mathrm{scale}}$-block blindness on homogeneity-preserving mutators—is confirmed on 5/6 SUTs admitting the relevant MR, with the sixth's exception accounted for by the prediction's quantitative tail") would meaningfully strengthen the paper's empirical contribution claim.

### 已知小瑕疵

- **A-M1. Table 7 (line 1597-1605) reports $n_G = 11$ but the prose at line 2073 says "$9/11$ vs $2/11$" on $G$, which matches Table 7. Cross-check on line 1222 says "Set~N $0/7$ on the two Euclidean-style SUTs" — both correct but the "7" sub-denominator (Set-G-only kills) is repeatedly conflated with the D2 stratum denominator (also coincidentally 5 → "7 G-only kills" in some prose, "5 D2 mutants" in others). Use a consistent symbol policy: $n_b$ for per-block denominator, $n_{D2}$ for D2 stratum size, and never share "7" between the two.

- **A-M2. Theorem 1 (closure) is "near-tautological" by the paper's own admission (line 383).** The paper handles this transparently. The companion claim that "Theorem 1 converts an empirical-adequacy claim into a structural-adequacy claim" is acceptable, but a single-sentence summary in the abstract would help. Currently the abstract leaves the impression Theorem 1 is more substantive than the paper itself admits §3.3 it is.

- **A-M3. Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:seven-blocks} is named "eight-block sufficiency" but the *internal* label is `hyp:seven-blocks` (line 282-287).** Cosmetic; the LaTeX label was added before the eighth block ($\mathcal{B}^{*}_{\mathrm{rel}}$) was added. Renaming to `hyp:eight-blocks` would harmonise.

- **A-M4. The `B_rel` block extends the framework beyond the Lie-group / self-adjoint / time-reversal core, but its non-emptiness is demonstrated only on relational query optimisers (§4.4).** The case is solid in itself; minor risk is that a reviewer reads it as a single-instance afterthought. A one-line forward reference from the §2.8 definition to §4.4 would tighten the structure.

- **A-M5. The "ten pairwise-independent Translate-extension dimensions" claim (abstract, line 78; introduction line 134; §4.4 line 827) is structurally novel but the supporting catalogue is in three companion artefacts (theory/*.md). Reviewers cannot verify "ten" without consulting external files.** A single table in Appendix C.6 summarising the ten with one-line descriptors would make the count auditable.

## B. Reviewer 2 ARS (5 维度严苛审视)

### 1. 方法论缺陷

- **B1-PB1. SUT selection is concentrated on a *single* codebase (`MathSignalClass` + `ComplexSignal`).** §6.4 line 1264 states the 10 SUTs are "midpoint, exactLog2, isSequence, clamp, signum, ComplexSignal.add, gcdSig, lcmSig, hypotSig, powerSig" — all drawn from one codebase. §11.1 line 2102 acknowledges this transparently ("the 10 SUTs of §6.4 are concentrated on a single codebase") and points to the Apache Commons Math 3.6.1 pilot (3 SUTs, 5 MRs, 77 mutants) as cross-codebase replication. But the pilot is *n=3* (the Set G structurally N/A on Maven-resolved substrates, so head-to-head is absent), reports a single $G$-block kill rate (6/21 = 28.6% with Wilson 95% CI [13.8, 50.0]), and the $\mathcal{L}^*$ block carries $n=0$ on commons-math because no PIT mutator on bilinear SUTs breaks the bilinearity invariant (lines 2102 explains this is "the framework's structural prediction, not a measurement gap"). At n=3 SUTs the cross-codebase generalisation claim is descriptive at best. The §6.13 threats (e) and §11.1 external-validity paragraph correctly flag this; the Reviewer-2 reading is that the *primary* substrate is one codebase and the *cross-codebase* claim is supported by an underpowered pilot — and Set G coverage is structurally absent on the only cross-codebase result, so even the head-to-head story is single-codebase. This is severe but not blocker because the paper acknowledges it (§11.1 line 2102 final sentence: "The replication tests scope-internal generalisation, not whether the framework should apply to programs lacking explicit operator-algebraic structure"). Recommended fix: explicitly de-rate the C4 contribution claim in the abstract from "cross-codebase external validity" to "single-codebase substrate plus n=3 cross-codebase pilot, both underpowered".

- **B1-S1. Set N is hand-derived by a single author (line 1281, line 2100).** The paper mitigates this with a multi-LLM second-rater protocol: Cohen's κ 0.927–0.929 between author and each of three LLMs, Fleiss' κ 1.000 on items where all three parsed (line 2100). The LLM-shared-training-data caveat is correctly noted. This is acceptable for pre-industrial-port phase. Reviewer 2 will, however, observe that a *human* second rater is not provided; the κ values are LLM↔author, not human↔human. The §11.1 plan ("A human-pair κ replication is committed for the industrial-port phase of follow-up work") is appropriate but defers a real risk.

- **B1-S2. The protocol of §5.6 (lines 762-786) commits 5 MR sets against MR-Scout, GenMorph, three LLM-assisted methods (Shin / GPTMR / AutoMT), DeepCrime ML benchmarks, and e3nn / PyG real-bug mining — but the actual execution in the paper is limited to: GP-evolved (GenMorph) at 30-min budget on 8 SUTs; LLM-assisted Set L = single GPT-4 sample in §5.6 *and then* the (d.set-l) 2-vendor × 5-temp ensemble in §6.10 (487 proposals → 212 translated, 43.5% template-match coverage); MR-Scout "adapted estimate" not full re-execution; DeepCrime real-fault pilot at n=5; e3nn / PyG real-bug evaluation = protocol only.** The (b) full D4J extension, (b.cm) commons-math extension, (c) GenMorph upstream patch, (d.scout) MR-Scout re-execution, (d.set-l-claude) third-vendor Claude replication, and the real-bug evaluation (lines 779-787) are all "committed future work". This is more deferral than a final TOSEM submission should carry. Required: the SOTA-baseline comparison is partial (1.5 of 3 categories fully executed; 1 of 3 partially through ensemble); the abstract's "comparative evaluation against three SOTA representatives" is technically true but reads stronger than the partial state.

### 2. 外部效度

- **B2-PB1 (already covered in B1-PB1).** Single-codebase substrate with n=3 cross-codebase pilot.

- **B2-S1. PUT (program under test) selection is pre-registered (line 1248), which is good, but the pre-registration criterion is package-rooted on `org.apache.commons.math3.{ode, linear, transform, analysis.solvers, distribution, optim, fitting, stat.regression, complex, geometry, fraction}` — yet the actual 10 SUTs are drawn from a custom `MathSignalClass + ComplexSignal` toy substrate, not these commons-math packages directly.** Lines 1281 and 2102 do clarify that commons-math 3.6.1 is the cross-codebase pilot, and `MathSignalClass` etc. is the primary substrate, but a reader is left to reconcile. Recommended: explicit cross-reference in §6.4 line 1248 paragraph.

- **B2-S2. The DeepCrime-style pilot (§5.6.1, n=5) uses 5 mutation operators on the trained EGNN checkpoint.** The paper's framing ("we executed a n=5 pilot extension of the case study", line 736) and verdict ("Set N detects 2/5 cat-v mutations [vs L,B 0/5]; the direction is consistent with the framework's prediction"; line 755 reports Fisher exact p=1.00, correctly noting underpowered) is acceptable per C6 rule. But this pilot is then folded into C4's "cross-domain transferability" claim in the introduction (line 136), where it appears as a positive bullet. Reviewer 2 reads this as 2/5 ≠ 0/5 ≠ 0/5 being dressed as evidence, when the proper reading is "direction observed; n=5 insufficient for inference". The line 136 text does say "underpowered for α=0.05 inferential conclusions" — so the construction is correct, just dense. Tighten to a one-sentence stand-alone reading.

### 3. 统计选择偏差

- **B3-PB1. HARKing risk in the n=20 case-study cat-(iv) result (Table 4 line 706).** The construct-validity caveat at lines 716-717, 720, 759-760 admits this *explicitly*: "the mutation set was constructed to cover one defect category per non-empty block of $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{equi}}$; in particular, cat-(iv) was selected because it targets the $\mathcal{T}^*$ block that $\rho_{\mathrm{train\text{-}rev}}$ alone covers. The 5/5 unique-detection result therefore exhibits construct validity of $\rho_{\mathrm{train\text{-}rev}}$ as a gradient-reversal probe rather than NOETHER's superiority". This is intellectually honest. The Reviewer-2 concern is that the Fisher p = 0.008 / McNemar p = 0.016 still appear in the prose (line 714), so a casual reader of §5.6 not arriving at line 716 reads "statistically significant superiority". For a TOSEM polish, recommend moving the p-values *after* the construct-validity caveat, or printing them with a parenthetical "(construct-validity-controlled; see paragraph below)".

- **B3-S1. The §6.10 pooled comparison reports McNemar exact two-sided $p = 0.0043$ on $n=57$ (D1+D2 pooled), and separately $p = 0.019$ on D1 alone, *both* against the framework**. The paper handles this correctly (lines 1809-1817) — it explicitly concedes Set G is stronger on the D1 scope-matched subset. The Reviewer-2 question is whether the framework's narrative should foreground the D1 loss in the abstract. See A-PB3.

- **B3-S2. Bonferroni / Holm correction is applied for the 8 × 2 = 16 paired comparisons in §6.10 (line 1515-1517).** Threshold $\alpha/16 \approx 0.003$; no per-SUT contrast meets it. This is correct stats practice; flagged here as positive.

- **B3-M1. Pre-registration is git-stamped (line 1252-1261) — the SHA-256 of the criterion file and matching commit hash are deposited in supplementary S7.** This is best-practice transparency. Reviewer-2-positive.

### 4. Benchmark 不公正

- **B4-PB1. GenMorph (Set G) is run at *its* published 30-min GAssert budget; Set N has no budget restriction.** This is the right comparison for a real-world cost-axis claim, but if Set G's GP search converged at 30 min, then *increasing* the budget would not change the answer; if it did not converge, then Set N's apparent loss on D1 is even larger. The §6.10 line 1903-1906 sensitivity rerun at 1-min budget (Set G drops from 40 to 39 pooled kills, with 1 SUT changing) suggests near-saturation at 30 min. Reviewer-2 reading: this is fair comparison. But the *cost-axis* claim in Table 8 ($\mathrm{poly}(|\mathrm{gen}|)$ vs 30-min wall) is contingent on the assumption that GP search is fundamentally needed; a hand-crafted MR set could match Set N's MR-generation cost at zero LLM token cost and zero search wall-time too. The cost claim is best read as "NOETHER replaces an iterative GP search with a deterministic poly-time construction", which is the §6.11 line 1996-1998 phrasing. OK.

- **B4-PB2. The LLM-assisted baseline Set L in §5.6 is a single GPT-4 sample at temperature 0 with one prompt** (line 760; "Set L is a single GPT-4 sample at temperature 0 with a fixed seed"). The §6.10 (d.set-l) ensemble of 2-vendor × 5-temperature × 487 proposals → 212 translated is a substantial improvement but is reported as Set L throughout §5.6 as the original single-sample baseline, with the upgraded ensemble appearing only in §6.10. A reviewer reading §5.6 first will reasonably think Set L is weak by construction. The §6.10 update at line 1729-1758 correctly supersedes the §5.6 sampling, but the §5.6 narrative is not retrospectively edited. Recommend: a single "Set L is superseded in §6.10" forward pointer at line 666 / 760.

- **B4-S1. MR-Scout (mining-based baseline) is reported via "an adapted estimate of MR-Scout's reach on the §6.4 substrate, derived from MR-Scout's published artifact reach figures rather than a full re-execution" (line 1842-1845).** The cost-axis interpretation in Table 8 is OK, but the absence of a full MR-Scout re-execution means the *detection* comparison against the mining-based category is incomplete. Acknowledged in (d.scout) of Table 9. This is a published gap; not concealed.

### 5. 霍桑效应

- **B5-OK. Not applicable.** The paper is a framework + empirical evaluation, not a behavioural / educational intervention. No human subjects, no longitudinal behaviour change, no participant awareness effect. The 5-dimension scan is complete in 4 dimensions; this one is empty.

## Reviewer notes (not action items)

- The paper's *epistemic discipline* is unusual for the genre: it explicitly carves out four boxed "Boundary of contribution" panels (lines 142-156, 440-442, 2195-2199), and the §3.3 Theorem 1 caveat (line 383) admits the "near-tautological" reading openly. This is much closer to the Reviewer-2-positive end of the spectrum than the genre average. The blockers identified here are real but recoverable: A-PB1 is an editing slip; A-PB2 and A-PB3 are tone / framing issues; B1-PB1 / B3-PB1 / B4-PB2 are either acknowledged in the existing §Threats and need re-surfacing, or are recoverable via post-acceptance follow-up work that is already committed in Table 9.

- The §6.5 `L*`-blindness result deserves more weight in the paper's headline claim than it currently receives. It is the only ex-ante, public-info-derivable, falsifiable, *confirmed* prediction in the paper; on 5/6 SUTs the kill rate is 0/N with mechanistic explanation for the 6th. This is a genuine empirical contribution.

- The §6.6 D1 / D2 / D2-prediction reading is mathematically clean (D1 is the framework's design target; D2 is the scope boundary). The execution is undermined by the n=5 / 0.434-CI issue (P0.2) and the 0/5 / 0/7 inconsistency (P0.1), both of which are *repairable* without re-running any experiments. After repair, the D2 prediction reading is fine.

- The five PWR-counterexample obstructions (Table 6, §6.5) + five additional $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{equi}}$ / $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{rel}}$ candidates (§4.4 line 826) = "ten pairwise-independent Translate-extension dimensions" is a strong theoretical artefact in its own right. The honesty of the negative-instantiation strategy (deliberately exhibiting MRs *outside* the framework's reach on the framework's principal application domain) is unusual and reviewer-positive. Strengthen by tabulating all 10 in one place (P2.3).
