# What the home-field experiments establish — three detectability laws

This is the offensive reading of the S10 results. The point of the experiment is
not to report detection rates; per-SUT rates are small-n and we do not lean on
them. The point is to argue, from cross-SUT *structure*, that an operator's
symmetry algebra **determines** the fault-detection power of any oracle derived
from it. We state three propositions as strong, falsifiable claims, each with the
evidence that supports it and the single observation that would refute it.

---

## P1 — Block law: the MR battery is a function of the physics, not the tester

**Claim.** The metamorphic relations one can *derive* for a numerical operator are
generated by that operator's symmetry group and conservation structure. The
NOETHER "blocks" a battery populates are therefore a fingerprint of the equation,
fixed *before* any test is run, not an artifact of the tester's ingenuity.

**Evidence (9 SUTs, zero exceptions).** The reversible, energy-conserving wave
equation is the *only* SUT that populates **T_rev\*** and it also populates
**Conservation**; every dissipative or steady operator (heat, Poisson, advection-
diffusion, radiation diffusion, Gray–Scott, detonation, combustion, pin-cell)
leaves **T_rev\*** empty. Conservation appears exactly where a conserved quantity
exists (wave, advection-diffusion mass, reaction balances) and is absent in the
mass-losing Dirichlet heat / steady Poisson solves. The block pattern is
*predictable from the PDE class alone*.

**Refutation.** Exhibit a dissipative operator that admits a valid (pristine-
passing, baseline-clean) time-reversal MR, or a genuinely conservative operator
whose battery cannot express a conservation MR. Either kills P1.

---

## P2 — Invariance-blindness law: a single oracle family is structurally incomplete

**Claim.** An MR that is *valid because* the operator is invariant under a
transformation T is, for that exact reason, **blind** to any fault that only
perturbs motion along T's orbit. The kernel of an oracle's detection map contains
the faults living in the symmetry the oracle exploits. Detection completeness
therefore requires a *family* of oracles whose **joint invariance-kernel is
trivial**; no single symmetry-based battery can be complete.

**Evidence (paired, same 29 real mutants).** The algebra-MR battery and the
neutral cross-implementation differential oracle detect 13/29 and 12/29
respectively, but they are **complementary, not redundant**: MR-only = 6,
differential-only = 5 (McNemar exact p = 1.0).

- The MR battery missed **every advection-speed error** (`fv_adv_speed_x2`,
  `sp_adv_speed_x2`) and every wavenumber/symbol-sign fault. These live in the
  translation / Galilean symmetry — the precise invariance that *validates* the
  battery's advection MRs. The battery is blind to them by construction, and we
  *predicted* this before tabulating it.
- The differential oracle caught those (differential-only is dominated by
  speed and symbol-sign faults) because it exploits no such invariance.
- Conversely, the MR battery caught conservation, boundary, and inhomogeneity
  faults (MR-only = 6) whose field perturbation falls below the differential
  oracle's tolerance but breaks a structural invariant.

**Refutation.** Show a symmetry-based MR that reliably detects faults purely along
its own invariance orbit (e.g., a Galilean-invariant MR that flags advection-
speed errors), or show that the two oracles' detected sets are nested rather than
crossing. Either kills P2. The claim is that metamorphic testing's blind spot is
*principled*, so the field's reliance on MR batteries alone is structurally
incomplete — the remedy is oracles of complementary symmetry, not more MRs of the
same class.

---

## P3 — Discretisation detectability floor: a hard impossibility, not an engineering gap

**Claim.** For differential testing across two discretisations with legitimate
cross-implementation gap δ, **any fault whose field signature is smaller than δ is
undetectable by every false-positive-free differential oracle**, independent of
tolerance design. δ is computable a priori and bounds differential-oracle recall.

**Evidence.** With initial-amplitude normalisation, δ = 0.185 (max over 5 probes)
between M-FV and M-SP. A doubled-diffusion fault (`fv_lap_coeff_x2`) sits *above*
the floor (signature 0.31) and is recoverable with a margin as tight as 1.5·δ
(0 baseline false positives). But faults with signature ≤ δ — half-coefficient
(0.068), θ-scheme drift (0.147), Nyquist drop (0.185) — cannot be separated from
discretisation noise: pushing τ below δ trips the baselines. The sensitivity
sweep confirms recall rises monotonically as the margin tightens toward δ and
stops there with the floor faults still invisible.

**Refutation.** Detect a sub-δ fault with a tolerance regime that keeps baseline
false positives at zero. That kills P3.

---

## Scope, stated as boundary not apology

These are structural claims argued from cross-SUT pattern, paired comparison, and
an a-priori-computable floor — none of which depend on a single small-n rate. The
per-SUT detection rates (and their Wilson intervals) are reported for
completeness and are underpowered individually; they are not the argument and
should not be read as the contribution. The differential oracle's own blind spot
— common-mode faults in code shared by both implementations — is real and is the
dual of P2 for the differential family; here every mutation targets one
implementation, so that regime is unexercised and the claim about it is, for now,
a prediction rather than a result.

We invite the obvious attacks: that the blocks are an artifact of *our* battery
(P1), that a cleverer single oracle is complete (P2), that a smarter tolerance
beats the floor (P3). Each has a one-line refutation above. That is the point.
